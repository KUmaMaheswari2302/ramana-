Publisher’s Note

It is a commonly held belief that spiritual progress can be made and karma worked
 off only during a human life. However, Sri Bhagavan taught and proved to
  devotees that it is also possible for animals to work off their karma. Once he

said, “We do not know what souls may be tenanting these bodies and for finishing
what part of their unfinished karmas they seek our company.”

Sri Bhagavan showed the same consideration for the animals as for the people
whom a kind destiny had brought to his feet. And animals behaved like humans with
him. He never referred to an animal in the normal Tamil way as ‘it’ but always as ‘he’
or ‘she’, and talked to them as he would to a human person. Truly he knew them and
they him. It was a regular Ashram rule that at mealtimes the dogs were fed first, then
any beggars who came, and finally the devotees. On the day he left his body, when the
doctors said that the pain must be frightful, Bhagavan heard peacocks calling plaintively
nearby and asked whether they had received their food.1

Among the devotees of Bhagavan who experienced the bliss of his presence, Valli
the Deer, Jackie the Dog and Cow Lakshmi are noteworthy because Bhagavan had

samadhis constructed for all three of them. And while it is believed that liberation can
be bestowed on any devotee in the presence of Bhagavan, the case of Cow Lakshmi is
outstanding: Bhagavan himself declared that Cow Lakshmi — like his mother — had
attained mukthi and he even composed a Tamil venba celebrating her liberation.

The following is the true story of a great soul who took birth as a humble cow and
gained supreme liberation through the grace of her teacher, Bhagavan Sri Ramana
Maharshi. Intended for youngsters, it has been simplified and dramatized. Where the
historical record is bare, dialogues step into the breach and tell us what might have been
said at a given moment in time. While the chronological sequence of the original
events cannot be confirmed with certainty, the incidents themselves are factual and
have their source in the numerous testimonies of the Ashram residents who witnessed
them firsthand, not least of all Bhagavan himself. In reading this account, let us
consider it from a child’s point of view, a child who wants to hear a good story. In that
spirit, let us set aside scholarly objectives and move into that domain so cherished by
children — the arena of story-telling.

Chapter 1
Journey of Hope

One night, a humble villager from the Tamil town of Kumaramangalam
near Gudiyatam had a vivid dream. In it he was directed to offer his
cow’s next calf at the feet of Bhagavan Sri Ramana Maharshi, the sage

of Arunachala. Being a man of faith, the villager did not doubt the truth of his
dream, but at that time he did not have the money to travel to Tiruvannamalai, the
temple town where Sri Ramana resided. Besides, as yet he had no calf to offer.

Not long after this, however, as if by divine decree, his cow became pregnant and
gave birth to a beautiful young calf. Shortly thereafter, his fortunes improved and he
lost no time in fulfilling the instruction of his dream. Not only did he now intend to
offer Sri Ramana his newborn calf, but the mother cow as well.

So one Friday afternoon in December 1926, four years after Sri Bhagavan
Ramana moved down to the foot of Arunachala, this man arrived at the Ashram
with his grandson after a long journey. He was filled with the hope that Sri
Bhagavan would accept this token of his devotion.

Outside the hall, some residents were gathered. Among them were two youngsters
and their father as well as Ramanathan Brahmachari, a humble devotee of Bhagavan
Ramana. All gazed in wonder as strangers approached with a cow and calf…

Chapter 2
Daily Visits

Ramanathan Brahmachari was a model devotee — faithful, modest, and
sincere. He had such love for Bhagavan that Bhagavan once said he
 could  not resist his wishes. The saying, “When you attain true devotion,

you have found the rope with which to bind God” had relevance in the case of
Pillai’s cow and calf, for, while Arunachalam Pillai’s stubbornness and resolve had
been factors, it was Ramanathan’s childlike innocence and good intentions that
had won Bhagavan’s heart in the end.

Now, back in those days, the forest around the Ashram was abundant. Briar,
thistle and sagebrush as well as palmyra, vilva and neem trees were plentiful, as
were recently cultivated fruit-bearing trees. The abundant vegetation provided
refuge for the wild leopards that roamed the mountain, and for whom a cow and
calf might be an attraction. Moreover, as the two newcomers were allowed to
roam freely, they posed a threat to the Ashram gardens. For the first few months,

the cow and calf remained in the custody of Ramanathan Brahmachari. But due
to his many duties, he had to eventually turn over his job to a man in town.

A year passed uneventfully until one eclipse day in December, this caretaker
visited the Ashram, bringing with him the mother and her calf. The calf, nearly
full-grown, fixed her attention on Bhagavan. Bhagavan, in turn, gazed at her...

BHAGAVAN: What’s this, Ramanathan? A cow and calf came once before.
RAMANATHAN: Bhagavan, this is the same cow and calf that Arunachalam Pillai
made as an offering. This is Pasupathi Iyer who is looking after them for us.
PASUPATHI: Yes, Bhagavan. In my past life I must have earned some merit to be
entrusted with this cow and calf.
BHAGAVAN: Good, very good. You are caring for them very nicely. The calf has
developed well and is quite beautiful.

(Bhagavan strokes the calf. She hops about excitedly, goes round Bhagavan and
begins licking his hand.)

PASUPATHI: Look! What a sight. It’s going around Bhagavan with such devotion.
BHAGAVAN: Don’t say, “It’s going around,” say, “She’s going around”. Aren’t animals
living beings and, in reality, the Self?
PASUPATHI: Forgive me, Bhagavan, a slip of the tongue. The golden hand of
Bhagavan has touched this calf. How blessed she is!
BHAGAVAN: What do you do in town?
PASUPATHI: I run a dairy, Bhagavan.
BHAGAVAN: Caring for cows is no easy task, is it? But you are aptly named,
Pasupathi being the Lord of Animals. Where are you coming from just now?
PASUPATHI: Pali Tirtham. Today being a solar eclipse, we came to have our baths
and to show Bhagavan his cow and calf, and to have his darshan.

(While Bhagavan and Pasupathi talk, the calf explores the Ashram and then
returns. Finally, Pasupathi prostrates in reverence to Bhagavan and departs
with the mother cow and calf. Bhagavan goes inside and others follow.)

Growing Up

Lakshmi’s mother passed away soon
afterwards. By 1930, Lakshmi was full-
grown and a mother herself. Her habit of

coming and going each day led to plans for shifting
her permanently to the Ashram.

Even though she spent her evenings in town, she

One day, Bhagavan was answering the questions of devotees seated around

the hall and came to Bhagavan’s side...

BHAGAVAN: What… Lakshmi…? Oh, I see. It must be eleven o’clock.
ATTENDANT: (Looking at the wall clock) Yes, Bhagavan, it’s already eleven.
BHAGAVAN: If Lakshmi appears, we can be sure of the time.

(Bhagavan rises and, with Lakshmi leading, all go to the dining hall.)

Evening Departures

One afternoon late in her pregnancy, at the customary time of her
departure, Lakshmi came to the hall. Bhagavan was reading the newspaper.

She stood near him and started licking the paper. Finally, when his mild protests
went unheeded, Bhagavan laid aside the paper and put his hands behind Lakshmi’s
horns, resting his head against hers. Remaining like this for some time, Lakshmi
became motionless…

BHAGAVAN: (Looking up at a devotee nearby and speaking in a soft voice) Do you
know what Lakshmi is doing? (Devotee shakes her head while Lakshmi remains
completely still as if in deep, gentle bliss.) She’s in samadhi!

(After a few minutes, Bhagavan changes his position…)

BHAGAVAN: Lakshmi, how do you feel now?

(Lakshmi moves backwards, making her way reverently out of the hall, as if not
wanting to turn her tail toward Bhagavan.)

Special Days

Since ancient times, both the cow and Goddess Lakshmi have been revered
and are often linked. Lakshmi, the Goddess of wealth and abundance, is
said to be the strength of the Gods, the brightness of the sun and moon,

and the power of the planets. When the Gods churned the ocean of milk, the
nectar that resulted was Lakshmi herself. And when Vishnu incarnated as Surya,
Lakshmi rose up from the lotus.

Once, Brahma the Creator took the form of a cow in order to nourish humans
with his nectar — milk from the cow’s udder. Thus cows are worshipped as the
givers and sustainers of life — as is Goddess Lakshmi.

These hallowed beliefs still hold true today and are expressed in a number of
ways all over India, not least in the association of Friday — the day of Holy Mother
— with the worship of the cow. At Sri Ramanasramam this was beautifully shown
in the special attention Cow Lakshmi received on this day each week when she was
bedecked with garlands of jasmine and tulsi. As soon as she came for darshan in
full regalia, Bhagavan would note that Friday had arrived...

49



Mattu Pongal Day

The annual celebration of Mattu Pongal is the harvest festival dedicated to
cows, honouring and adorning them with garlands and tilakam, the vermillion

dot placed at the eyebrow centre. On this day, special puja is performed to Nandi
— Lord Siva’s bull — as well as to cows, oxen and buffaloes. All are offered boiled
sweet rice (pongal) and praised in sacred chant. In the evening, festoons of aloe
fibre and cloth with coins are attached to the horns of cows and oxen, who are
then jubilantly paraded through the streets of the town.

Chapter 7
Lakshmi Falls Ill

With the progress of time, the life force of all creatures, whether
animal or human, must eventually leave the body and return to its
source. In the early 1930s, Jackie the Dog expired peacefully in

Bhagavan’s arms. So did Valli the Deer who, wounded by hunters, breathed her
last late one night in the dining hall. In January 1947, Ramanathan Brahmachari
passed away, sending a wave of sadness throughout the community. Then, in
mid-1948, just six months before doctors discovered a small growth on Bhagavan’s
left elbow, Lakshmi fell ill.

Lakshmi had always enjoyed good health. She had been energetic and vibrant
all her life. But at twenty-two, she had now exceeded a cow’s life-expectancy. It
was hoped that she would recover quickly but, in fact, her condition steadily
deteriorated. A sombre mood pervaded the Ashram. Suri Nagamma, who had
grown fond of Lakshmi over the years, was on hand to care for her. In the
gosala…

NAGAMMA: (To a devotee) Lakshmi is not well at all. She hasn’t eaten in days.
DEVOTEE: Does Bhagavan know?

Chapter 8
Mahanirvana

At 10-30am, the doctor came again and gave an injection, but Lakshmi
remained unaffected, as though the body was no longer hers. There was

no suffering and her sight was clear and calm. He turned her over into the
posture of Nandi in order to apply some ointment on a boil. At 11-30,
Venkataratnam returned to find Nagamma still there. After a few minutes, with
Nagamma close by, Lakshmi took her last breath, finally reaching the feet of
Ramana Arunachala, to be absorbed in him forever. Meanwhile Bhagavan came
quietly out of the dining hall and returned to the gosala where devotees had
gathered...

BHAGAVAN: Is it over? (Those present gaze in silence as Bhagavan kneels and takes
Lakshmi’s face in both hands) Oh Lakshmi, Lakshmi! (Then, through his tears...)
Because of her, our family has grown to such an extent.

(All present made silent gestures of praise.)

BHAGAVAN: Do you see this? Her right ear is uppermost. Until yesterday she

Day of Mourning

Throughout the day, devotees came to pay their respects, bringing flowers,
offering prayers, and sprinkling holy water over Lakshmi’s body. The air in

the gosala was charged with a vibrant serenity. Some devotees stood at a distance
and discussed in whispers the circumstances of Lakshmi’s passing…

ASHRAMITE: When the life of Bhagavan’s mother was ebbing, Bhagavan did exactly
the same.
DEVOTEE: Is that so?
ASHRAMITE: Yes, he rested Mother’s head on his lap. Then he placed his right hand
on her heart and his left on her head, remaining that way for quite some time.
DEVOTEE: Both are blessed to have been delivered at Bhagavan’s hands.

72



Final Rites

Meanwhile, work began at the burial site. A pit was dug next to the tombs
of the other animals. At 6-30 that evening, using a wooden cart,

Chinnaswami moved Lakshmi’s body to the burial site, just opposite to where
Bhagavan was sitting. Devotees brought pots of water to pour over the body,
while Bhagavan recounted Lakshmi’s life story. He praised her sweet affectionate
nature and intelligence, and observed…

BHAGAVAN: She might have been a sadhaka in the past, which earned her the
merit to be brought to the Ashram to attain liberation.

(Abhishekam is performed with milk, curd, ghee, sugar and rose water. Finally,
a silk cloth is draped over her. Lakshmi’s face, radiant and peaceful, shows no
sign of death. She is garlanded and anointed with turmeric and kumkum for
the last time.)

BHAGAVAN: It was the same in the case of Mother. Even until abhishekam was
done, the lustre of her face did not fade.

(The last rites are performed with the chanting of the Vedas. Gomatha Lakshmi,
the queen of her tribe, receives rites normally reserved for royalty. Her body,
hardly visible beneath the abundance of garlands and camphor, is slowly lowered

grave is then filled in with earth. Later that evening, after consulting the almanac,

In the year of Sarvadhari,
Fifth of Ani, a Friday,
In the waxing phase,

Twelfth lunar day, under Visakam,
Cow Lakshmi attained liberation.

Some Weeks Later...

One evening in late summer, the air was filled with the sound of
crickets and cicadas. After sitting silently in the hall for some time,

Bhagavan, as if reminded of something, got up and went out alone. On
the way, he met Nagamma…

BHAGAVAN: Come, Nagamma.
NAGAMMA: Bhagavan seems to be on his way to the gosala.
BHAGAVAN: Yes, I felt like seeing Lakshmi’s children.

(The two amble along slowly.)

BHAGAVAN: (Still at a distance, Bhagavan spots a large brown bull) Do you see
that big fellow? That’s Lakshmi’s son. (As they move closer) And this one here,

this is Lakshmi’s daughter. And that one, you see there…that’s Lakshmi’s

NAGAMMA: How is Bhagavan able to tell them apart?
BHAGAVAN: They all have Lakshmi’s face, don’t they? But isn’t it strange how

NAGAMMA: Yes, Bhagavan. (Pauses and reflects for a few moments) Everything

BHAGAVAN: It’s true that there has to be good karma. That alone enables the

BHAGAVAN: When Palaniswamy was breathing his last, I placed my hands on

NAGAMMA: It did?
BHAGAVAN: But when Mother was in the last stage, I kept my hands on her head

force had subsided, I continued to keep my hands on her for some time. All of

NAGAMMA: Bhagavan blessed Lakshmi in the same way.
BHAGAVAN: Yes, as with Mother, all her vasanas were done away with during the
final stage.
NAGAMMA: But Bhagavan did not remain with Lakshmi right up until her last
breath.
BHAGAVAN: Do you know why? No predispositions remained in her!
NAGAMMA: How blessed she was, Bhagavan. Graced by the touch of Bhagavan’s
hands in their final hours, both Mother and Lakshmi attained liberation!

Postscript

Today, more than fifty years later, puja is performed at Cow Lakshmi’s samadhi
every Friday morning. Each year on the anniversary of her liberation and

on Mattu Pongal Day, elaborate ceremonies are conducted there. Streams of
visitors continue to pay their respects, seeking the blessings and inspiration of this
great soul, so utterly devoted to Bhagavan.

Of the 120 cows currently living in the Ashram gosala, a significant number
are Lakshmi’s descendents.
